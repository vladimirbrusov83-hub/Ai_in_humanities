# Product

## Register

brand

## Users
Undergraduate and graduate humanities students, and the librarians who teach them.
They arrive via a syllabus link, a research guide, or a workshop, usually while
deciding how (or whether) to use AI in their own project. Secondary audience:
faculty writing an AI policy for a course or a department.

## Product Purpose
An interactive visual essay mapping ten phases of humanities research — question
through afterlife — showing who carries the load at each: the scholar (situated
judgment), agentic AI (bounded, verifiable assistance), and the academic library
(archive, access, permanence). Success is a student coming away able to say where
AI genuinely helps in *their* discipline, where it fails in ways specific to
text-based scholarship, and which parts of the work are the contribution itself.
Not a hype piece, and not an abstinence lecture: AI use is presented as a
spectrum, and every point on it is treated as a defensible position.

## Brand Personality
Field guide, not dashboard. Printed, considered, quietly authoritative — a
well-made reference book rather than a SaaS product page. Inherits the visual
identity of its sibling, The Research Lifecycle: warm serif display type, a deep
slate ground, editorial restraint over chrome.

## Anti-references
Not a SaaS landing page. Not a slide deck converted to web. Avoid dashboard
language (metric tiles, sidebar nav). Avoid the two easy tones: AI evangelism,
and moral panic. Where scholars genuinely disagree, present two positions and
let the reader weigh them — the contested zones are the point, not a caveat.

## Design Principles
- Read like a printed field guide; editorial pacing over dashboard density.
- Make the recursion visible. The spiral and the backward arrows are the argument:
  humanities research doubles back, and a linear map would be a lie.
- Show the mismatch rather than smoothing it. The page says out loud that it was
  adapted from a science model and that reflexivity doesn't belong at position 07.
- Give the library lane more visible weight than the science version does, because
  in the humanities the evidence itself is a library holding.
- Never fabricate an example that could survive being quoted. The worked
  fabricated citation names an invented scholar and an invented journal on purpose.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. All interactive elements — spiral nodes, zone
panels, phase accordions, both tools — are keyboard-operable with visible focus.
Body and secondary text verified against WCAG AA contrast on the page ground.
Plain-language glosses for interpretive-theory terms, so the page works for an
undergraduate who has never met the word "hermeneutic".
